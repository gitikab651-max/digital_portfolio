01 / BUSINESS MATHEMATICS · WAS GROUP 10
TAPMI · Semester 1 group project

Business Mathematics — IndiGo Break-Even Analysis

Financial modelling of airline capacity, operating costs and break-even performance.

Relevance: Business students and aviation analysts examining how fixed costs, fuel costs and revenue affect capacity decisions.

Problem / brief

Airlines carry high fixed costs irrespective of seat occupancy. Our project examined how much flying IndiGo would need to cover an adjusted cost base and how fuel or fare changes could shift that threshold.

Method & approach

Using the FY 2025–26 figures in the group presentation, we classified operating costs into fixed and variable components and modelled revenue, cost and surplus as linear functions of Available Seat Kilometres (ASK). The break-even equation was x* = F ÷ (p − v), supported by graphs, inequalities and sensitivity tests.

Findings / output

The model produced a break-even point of 158.46 billion ASK and a margin of safety of 8.11% at 172.443 billion ASK flown. A 20% fuel-cost rise raised the modelled break-even point to 175.41 billion ASK, beyond actual flying.

Scope

A simplified academic model based on FY26 company figures, not a prediction of reported profit. Forex losses and selected one-off items were excluded from the modelled cost base.

02 / BASICS OF ACCOUNTING · SECTION C / GROUP 10
TAPMI · Academic group project

Accountancy — HAL Accounting Cycle

A complete classroom accounting-cycle model grounded in HAL’s audited opening balances.

Relevance: Readers seeking to understand how source balances become journals, ledgers, trial balances and financial statements.

Problem / brief

Our group was assigned Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) to demonstrate an end-to-end accounting cycle, from historical opening balances to a complete set of closing statements.

Method & approach

We carried forward audited FY 2024–25 standalone opening balances, recorded 30 hypothetical FY 2025–26 transactions (15 general and 15 business-specific), prepared journal entries and ledger postings, and applied seven year-end adjustments before drafting statements.

Findings / output

The illustrative adjusted trial balance totalled ₹1,28,793.77 crore per side. The classroom model closed with a balanced statement of financial position of ₹1,12,367.24 crore and a simulated profit after tax of ₹913.21 crore.

Scope

The FY25 opening numbers were sourced from HAL’s audited standalone accounts. All FY26 transactions, adjustments, taxes and outcomes are expressly part of the academic simulation, not HAL’s actual reported FY26 results.

03 / TECHNOLOGY LITERACY FOR MANAGERS · MNAT 10
TAPMI · News-analysis brief

TLM — India’s Payment System Goes Global

Business and technology analysis of UPI’s architecture and international expansion.

Relevance: Fintech teams, merchants and decision-makers considering digital payment accessibility and international commerce.

Problem / brief

The course brief examined why India’s Unified Payments Interface (UPI) is expanding beyond domestic payments and what its globalisation means for businesses and financial inclusion.

Method & approach

The analysis was organised around three questions: what happened in UPI’s expansion, how its open interoperable architecture differs from private payment networks, and how Indian fintech businesses could serve travellers, diaspora communities and international trade.

Findings / output

The brief reports 185 billion transactions in FY 2024–25 and discusses international reach, network effects and public digital infrastructure. The managerial lens identifies cross-border payment acceptance and connected services as areas for further evaluation.

Scope

This is a technology-and-management news analysis, not a deployed payment product. Figures and expansion examples follow the course brief supplied for the assignment.

04 / LLR · RHETORICAL & COMMUNICATION ANALYSIS
TAPMI · Academic analysis

LLR — Sridhar Vembu: Building a Brand Persona

A rhetorical strategy focused on credibility, emotional resonance and persuasive reasoning.

Relevance: Students of rhetoric, communication and brand strategists interested in how leadership narratives shape public trust.

Problem / brief

The project examined how Sridhar Vembu’s public-facing brand persona could be communicated more effectively and how a coherent narrative could strengthen credibility and audience connection.

Method & approach

We structured the communication analysis around Aristotle’s three rhetorical appeals: ethos to foreground credibility and values, pathos to connect with audience concerns and aspirations, and logos to support the narrative through clear reasoning and evidence.

Findings / output

The proposed positioning framework integrates credibility, relatability and logical justification into one consistent leadership narrative. It offers a basis for evaluating speeches, profiles and public messaging.

Scope

A course-based rhetoric and brand-positioning exercise. No public campaign, audience testing or measured reputation change is claimed.

05 / DIR · DIVERSITY, INCLUSIVITY & ROOTEDNESS
TAPMI · Ethnographic field study

DIR — AI and Art: An Ethnographic Study

Seven artist interviews exploring creative identity, professional livelihoods and emerging technology.

Relevance: Artists, educators and cultural practitioners exploring technological change through an inclusive, people-centred lens.

Problem / brief

We investigated how artists define creative work, where AI enters their process and what concerns arise about originality, consent, recognition and employment.

Method & approach

The study used seven semi-structured interviews with participants at Srishti Manipal Institute, Bengaluru. We reviewed anonymised responses (I1–I7) and fieldnotes, organised findings into themes and connected them to the DIR principles of diversity, inclusivity and rootedness.

Findings / output

Participants described art as personal expression, everyday practice and livelihood. They identified uses for AI in research, feedback and idea refinement, while also raising concerns about creative confidence, freelance work, consent and recognition. A recurring position was that technology should support—not replace—human creativity.

Scope

Qualitative findings reflect the perspectives of seven interviewees at one field site; they are not intended as a representative survey of all artists.
